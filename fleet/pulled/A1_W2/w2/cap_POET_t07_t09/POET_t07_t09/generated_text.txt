The dust hangs thick in the air,
a golden haze that smells of heat and ache.
The grass is brittle, broken glass underfoot,
each blade a tiny, dry tongue lapping at the sun.

A wind picks up,
not gentle, but frantic,
whipping the stalks into a frenzy,
scattering seeds like confetti.

Darkness rolls in from the west,
a bruised purple bruise spreading across the sky.
The light turns flat,
the shadows deepening like bruises on the earth.

A low rumble,
the earth's own stomach growling.
The air grows heavy,
thick with the promise of what’s to come.

The first drop falls,
a cold kiss on the hot cheek of the world.
Then another.
And another.
