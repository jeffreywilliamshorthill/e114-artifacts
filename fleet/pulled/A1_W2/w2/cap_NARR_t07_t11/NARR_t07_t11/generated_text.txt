The foghorn groaned, a long, mournful sound that vibrated through the stones of the lighthouse and into the keeper's bones. It was the only thing that could be heard over the relentless slap of waves against the jagged rocks below, the rhythmic pulse of the sea that had been his constant companion for twenty years.

Keeper Elias Thorne stood in the lantern room, his breath fogging the thick glass as he peered out into the pale, grey light of dawn. The sky was a bruised purple, streaked with hints of orange that promised a day neither warm nor cold, but damp and indifferent. He had seen countless dawns from this vantage point, each one blending into the next in a cycle of solitude and duty. But today, something was wrong.
