The air thickens, a heavy wool blanket
pressed against the skin.
The dust, usually a pale, choking ghost,
settles into a deep, brown silence.

The grass, brittle and straw-colored,
stops its frantic, dry rattling.
It holds its breath,
leaning into the sudden, electric hush.

A single, dark bruise blooms on the horizon,
swelling with a weight that the earth can no longer hold.
The light turns a strange, bruised violet,
stripping the world of its color,
leaving only the anticipation of the coming wash.

The wind dies in the throat of the valley.
The birds have gone to ground.
The field waits,
a parched mouth
opening to the sky.
