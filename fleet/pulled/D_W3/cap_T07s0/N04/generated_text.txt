Bees attending to their own hive are engaged in a complex and fascinating array of activities that are crucial for the survival and functioning of the colony. Here's an honest look at what it's like to observe bees in action:

### Bee Activities in the Hive

1. **Foraging**: Worker bees leave the hive to collect nectar, pollen, water, and propolis from flowers and other sources. This is essential for feeding the colony and making honey.

2. **Hive Maintenance**: Bees clean and maintain the hive structure. They repair and expand the wax combs where eggs are laid, larvae are raised, and honey is stored.

3. **Nurse Bees**: Young worker bees act as nurse bees, feeding larvae and ensuring they grow properly. They secrete royal jelly, which is the primary food for young larvae.

4. **Queen Care**: Worker bees take care of the queen bee, feeding her and keeping her clean. They also help in the development of new queens during swarming events.

5. **Thermoregulation**: Bees regulate the temperature inside the hive by fanning their wings to cool the hive or clustering together to warm it.
