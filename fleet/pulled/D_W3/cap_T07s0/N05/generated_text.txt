Inside the hive, the air is still and warm, thick with the scent of wax and the faint, sweet musk of thousands of bees. It is a world of constant, purposeful vibration.

Here is how the processing of nectar happens, step by step, transforming the garden's flower into the colony's golden fuel.

### 1. The Transfer: From Crop to Cell
It begins with the arrival of the **forager** bees. They return from the garden, their legs dusted with pollen, carrying nectar stored in their "honey stomachs" (a specialized crop used only for transport).

They do not dump the nectar into a vat. Instead, they rely on a biological handshake. A **receiver bee** (often a younger worker) emerges from the brood area. The forager extends her proboscis, and the receiver sucks the nectar into her own stomach. This exchange can happen dozens of times in succession.

> **Why?** This isn't just storage; it is the first stage of processing. Every time the nectar moves from bee to bee, enzymes (specifically invertase) are added to the mix. These enzymes begin the chemical work of breaking down the complex sucrose in the flower
