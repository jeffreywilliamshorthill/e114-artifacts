Imagine you’re standing in a quiet garden, the air warm and buzzing with life. Beneath your feet, hidden in the earth or tucked into a tree hollow, is a beehive—a bustling, living city built by thousands of bees. At the heart of this colony lies a remarkable process: the transformation of nectar into honey. Here’s how it works, from the garden to the hive:

---

### **1. Nectar Collection: The Garden’s Sweet Bounty**
- **Foraging Bees:** Worker bees (female bees) leave the hive in search of nectar, a sugary liquid produced by flowers. They use their long, straw-like tongues (proboscis) to sip nectar from blossoms, storing it in a special sac called the "honey stomach" (not for digestion, but for transport).
- **Navigation:** Bees communicate the location of flowers to their hive mates through a "waggle dance," sharing the direction and distance of the nectar source. This ensures efficient foraging across the garden.
