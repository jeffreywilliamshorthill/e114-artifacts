Bees attend to their own hive with incredible precision and dedication, driven by instinct and communication. Their behavior is a marvel of natural engineering, and the "hum" of their wings is an integral part of their daily operations.

### How Bees Attend to Their Hive:

1. **Division of Labor**:  
   Bees have specialized roles within the hive. Worker bees perform various tasks depending on their age:
   - Younger bees (nurse bees) care for the brood (eggs, larvae, and pupae), feeding them royal jelly or pollen.
   - Middle-aged bees clean cells, build wax comb, and guard the entrance.
   - Older bees forage for nectar, pollen, water, and propolis.

2. **Communication**:  
   Bees use a combination of pheromones, dances, and vibrations to communicate:
   - **Pheromones**: Chemical signals regulate colony activities, such as alerting others to danger or marking food sources.
   - **Waggle Dance**: Foragers perform this complex dance to convey the direction and distance of food sources to other bees.
   - **Vibrations**: Bees use vibrations to signal needs, like swarming or queen
