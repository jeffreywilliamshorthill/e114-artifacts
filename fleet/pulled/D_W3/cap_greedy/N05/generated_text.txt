Imagine a beehive nestled in a garden, buzzing with life as bees tirelessly process nectar into honey. Here's what that intricate process looks like for the colony:

### **1. Gathering Nectar**
Worker bees venture out from the hive, their tiny bodies laden with pollen baskets on their legs. They visit flowers, using their long tongues to sip sweet nectar and store it in their honey stomachs—a specialized organ separate from their digestive system. Each bee can carry up to 70% of its body weight in nectar.

### **2. Returning to the Hive**
Once full, the bees return to the hive, where they pass the nectar to house bees through a process called trophallaxis. This involves regurgitating the nectar into the mouths of other bees, who then continue passing it along until it reaches the cells of the honeycomb.
