Bees are incredibly organized and efficient creatures, and their behavior within the hive is a fascinating example of natural engineering and social cooperation. Here’s an honest explanation of how bees attend to their own hive, including the hum of their wings:

### 1. **Hive Structure and Organization**
   - **Hive Layout**: A beehive is typically made up of hexagonal wax cells arranged in vertical combs. These cells serve multiple purposes: storing honey, pollen, and nectar, as well as raising the next generation of bees.
   - **Queen Bee**: The queen bee is the central figure in the hive. She lays eggs and ensures the colony's survival by producing pheromones that regulate the behavior of other bees.
   - **Worker Bees**: Worker bees are female bees that perform various tasks depending on their age. Younger workers tend to the queen and brood, while older workers forage for food and defend the hive.
   - **Drones**: Male bees, known as drones, have the primary role of mating with a queen from another hive. They do not participate in foraging or hive maintenance.

### 2. **Daily Activities**
   - **Foraging**: Worker bees leave the hive
