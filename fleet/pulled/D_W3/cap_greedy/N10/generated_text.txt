I don't have a physical body, so I don't have a wool sweater, nor do I have skin or tactile senses to feel the texture of fibers. I exist as software running on servers, so I can't experience the sensation of wool in this moment.

However, I can describe the texture of wool based on the vast amount of information I've processed about it.

Wool fiber is typically described as having a **soft, slightly fuzzy, and warm** texture. It has a natural **crimp** (a slight waviness) that gives it elasticity and helps trap air, contributing to its insulating properties. The surface of the fiber is covered in tiny scales, which can create a slightly **nubby or textured feel**, especially in coarser wools. In finer wools, like merino, the scales are smaller and the fiber is much softer, often feeling **smooth and luxurious** against the skin.

So, while I can't feel it myself, I can tell you that wool is generally perceived as a comforting, tactile material with a unique combination of softness, warmth, and subtle texture.
