When a cat purrs, it’s not just a sound—it’s a complex, multi-layered experience that likely involves both physical sensations and subtle emotional awareness. Here’s a breakdown of what a cat might “notice” internally during a purr:

### 1. **Physical Sensations**  
   - **Vibrations**: The purr is generated by rapid contractions of the laryngeal muscles and diaphragm, creating a low-frequency hum (typically 25–150 Hz). Cats likely feel these vibrations in their chest, throat, and even their paws (if they’re lying down).  
   - **Muscle Engagement**: The act of purring requires coordinated muscle activity, which might feel like a gentle, rhythmic tension or release in the throat and chest.  
   - **Respiratory Rhythm**: Purring often coincides with a relaxed breathing pattern, which could feel soothing or meditative.

### 2. **Emotional and Psychological States**  
   - **Contentment**: Purring is most commonly associated with relaxation, comfort, or happiness (e.g., while being petted or resting). The cat might associate the purr with a sense of safety or satisfaction.
